2. Executive Summary

**Model ID:** IRIS-001

IRIS (Intelligent Research and Information System) is an advanced AI-powered research and response system initially designed for RBC's Accounting Policy Group and now expanded to support the broader CFO Group. It combines a sophisticated agent-based architecture with a comprehensive knowledge base to provide intelligent, accurate responses to finance and accounting policy inquiries through a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) approach.

Business Context and Purpose

IRIS serves the broader CFO Group by providing a centralized platform for finance and accounting policy inquiries, enabling users to obtain accurate, policy-compliant guidance through natural language conversations. While initially focused on accounting policies for APG, the system has evolved to address a wider range of finance-related policy information across the CFO Group. 

The system is designed to analyze incoming queries to determine the appropriate processing path and research relevant finance and accounting policies, standards, and guidance across multiple internal and external sources. It generates comprehensive, properly cited responses with appropriate disclaimers, supporting both straightforward inquiries and complex finance/accounting policy questions. This centralized approach allows for the addition of new databases to the existing model framework rather than building each individual RAG database as a separate chatbot, significantly improving efficiency and scalability in accessing critical financial policy information throughout the organization.

Modeling Approach

IRIS implements a sophisticated agent-based architecture that processes queries through two primary paths. The Direct Response Path handles queries that can be answered using only conversation history, following a minimal processing pipeline from Router to Direct Response Agent. For more complex inquiries, the Research Path activates a full processing pipeline: Router → Clarifier → Planner → Database Subagents → Summarizer.

The system operates on a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework, combining specialized agents with access to both internal RBC accounting knowledge and external authoritative sources. This approach ensures that responses are grounded in documented policies rather than generated from the LLM's general knowledge, providing reliable and traceable information for finance professionals.

Key Model Features

At the core of IRIS is a modular Agent-based architecture designed for specialized reasoning tasks. The Agent Router functions as the system gateway, determining the optimal processing path for each query. For research-intensive queries, the Agent Clarifier ensures sufficient context is available, while the Agent Planner strategically selects appropriate knowledge sources based on query analysis.

Specialized Database Subagents retrieve information from various sources using one of five distinct retrieval methodologies specifically engineered for different document types:

1. **Catalog Search - Small**: Used for standard documents (PDFs, DOCX files), employing a two-stage process with document catalog retrieval and LLM-based selection
2. **Catalog Search - Large**: Optimized for large documents with distinct sections/chapters, employing a three-stage process that includes section-level selection
3. **Catalog Search - Excel**: Custom approach for tabular data, preserving structured information from spreadsheets
4. **Catalog Search - Vision**: Specialized method for infographics and visual content, using vision models to extract information
5. **Semantic Search - Large**: Used for very large reference documents, employing vector-based similarity search with embedding technology

The subagents can process queries in two distinct modes based on user intent:
• **Metadata Search**: When users ask what documents are available on a topic, the system returns only document names and descriptions without retrieving full content or invoking the Summarizer
• **Research Mode**: When users request specific information on a topic, the system retrieves and processes the actual document content to generate comprehensive responses

For research-based queries, the Agent Summarizer synthesizes findings into coherent, cited responses. The entire system is supported by comprehensive process monitoring that tracks execution stages, token usage, and performance metrics to ensure operational reliability.

Knowledge Sources

The model accesses various knowledge sources structured in a tiered approach, with internal RBC documentation serving as primary authoritative sources. These include Corporate Accounting Policy Manuals, APG Cheat Sheet Infographics, APG Wiki Entries, Internal Accounting Memos, Project Approval Request Guidance, Internal Control over Financial Reporting Policy, and additional specialized internal knowledge bases. To supplement these, IRIS also integrates external sources including IASB Standards and Interpretations as well as guidance from select accounting firms (EY, KPMG, and PwC). This hierarchical approach ensures that internal policies take precedence while leveraging industry standards when appropriate.

Notable Limitations and Weaknesses

IRIS operates within specific boundaries that define its capabilities and limitations. The system can only provide information based on available knowledge sources and cannot leverage the LLM's general knowledge, which ensures responses are policy-compliant but limits coverage to documented topics. It is purpose-built for finance and accounting policy guidance and not designed for general-purpose uses or providing legal, tax, or regulatory filing advice. The quality of responses depends heavily on the availability and quality of source documents, with finite context windows limiting the amount of information processed in a single interaction. As with all LLM-based systems, there remains a risk of generating plausible-sounding but incorrect information, which necessitates appropriate disclaimers and verification statements for all responses.

Human-in-the-Loop and Risk Mitigation

IRIS implements a strong human-in-the-loop approach to ensure appropriate use. System access is strictly controlled through Active Directory groups, with users granted permissions to specific databases based on their role and responsibilities. Before using the system, all users must review and accept terms and conditions that outline the system's capabilities and limitations. 

The Maven user interface incorporates clear warnings about the importance of verifying information received, with appropriate disclaimers embedded in all responses. The system provides source references for all information, enabling users to verify content against original documents. By design, IRIS explicitly identifies situations where it cannot provide reliable information, preventing users from acting on incomplete or uncertain guidance. This multi-layered approach to risk mitigation ensures that the system serves as a research aid rather than replacing human judgment in critical finance decisions.

Compliance Framework

IRIS is not used for any regulatory purposes but functions as a search tool for users to retrieve information from documents. All outputs must be verified by users based on the source references provided. The system complies with RBC's Generative AI policies and Data Loss Prevention (DLP) requirements, ensuring appropriate handling of internal information.

The system incorporates multiple safeguards to clearly indicate when information represents general guidance rather than definitive advice and recommend verification with domain specialists before implementation. It maintains confidentiality of internal policies, identifies and refuses to answer queries outside its scope, and provides appropriate confidence signaling based on source authority and consensus. These mechanisms ensure that IRIS operates within established compliance frameworks and supports proper governance of financial information.

Model Materiality and Risk Assessment

IRIS is expected to deliver significant productivity benefits by providing faster access to finance and accounting policy information across the CFO Group. By implementing robust access controls, clear usage guidelines, and comprehensive documentation practices, the system demonstrates an ACCEPTABLE risk profile for internal usage, with an overall uncertainty level of ±10.4%. The system's design prioritizes accuracy and transparency, with clear attribution to source documentation and appropriate limitations on scope. These features, combined with ongoing monitoring and regular performance assessment, ensure that IRIS delivers value while maintaining the high standards required for financial information systems within RBC.

4. Model Structure and Approach

**Model Name:** IRIS (Intelligent Research and Information System)
**Model ID:** IRIS-001

4.1 Approach Selection

IRIS leverages large language models for both generative capabilities and embedding functions within a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework. Our approach to model selection was primarily driven by the models available within RBC's infrastructure:

1. Model Selection Constraints:
• Limited to models available through RBC's Lumina portal (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, GPT-4-turbo, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o)
• On-premises options (Llama, Qwen, Cohere) for specific use cases
• Not training our own models, focusing instead on optimal use of commercial options
• Performance on public benchmarks and leaderboards (MMLU, TruthfulQA)
• Context window length requirements to handle large documents
• Processing speed and cost efficiency considerations

2. Validation through internal testing:
• Initial samples provided to the APG team for feedback
• Internal review to confirm model sufficiency
• Assessment of response quality and accuracy for finance-specific queries
• Evaluation of citation quality and source attribution

Based on these constraints and evaluations, we selected GPT-4o-mini for quick decision-making and contexts where all necessary information is provided, GPT-4o for more important decisions and final response generation, and text-embedding-3-large for our embedding model. For visual content processing (infographics, images), we employ an on-premises hosted version of Qwen2 7B VL. For external data that cannot be redistributed, we use an on-premises version of Cohere command-north-large.

IRIS implements a modular, agent-based architecture with a hybrid RAG approach. The system processes user queries through a sequential pipeline with two primary processing paths:

1. **Direct Response Path**: For queries that can be answered using only conversation history
   * Agent Router → Agent Direct Response

2. **Research Path**: For queries requiring database research
   * Agent Router → Agent Clarifier → Agent Planner → Database Subagents → Agent Summarizer

Our agent-based approach was designed to be both conversational (using conversation history for context) and autonomous (searching specific databases as needed instead of all content at once). The architecture enables strategic querying of selected databases rather than querying all databases for every question, significantly improving efficiency and relevance.

After experimenting with multiple Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods, we developed a hybrid approach with five document processing pipelines for input and three specific retrieval methods:

**Five Input Processing Pipelines:**

1. **Catalog Search - Small**: Standard pipeline for processing normal documents (PDF, DOCX) that converts content to markdown and generates metadata.

2. **Catalog Search - Vision**: Specialized pipeline for processing infographics and visual documents using Qwen2 vision model to extract text, diagrams, and visual elements into markdown.

3. **Catalog Search - Excel**: Custom pipeline for converting Excel spreadsheets with structured data into markdown documents with preserved table formatting.

4. **Catalog Search - Large**: Pipeline for processing large documents with distinct sections, creating both document-level and section-level summaries.

5. **Semantic Search - Large**: Pipeline for very large reference documents that includes chunking, embedding generation, and metadata enrichment.

**Three Retrieval Methods:**

1. **Catalog Search - Small**: For documents processed through the Small, Vision, or Excel pipelines (under 10,000 tokens), we send entire documents within the context window rather than chunking. The model selects relevant documents from a catalog based on descriptions and metadata.

2. **Catalog Search - Large**: For larger documents with distinct sections, we implement a two-stage retrieval process where the model first selects relevant documents from a catalog and then identifies specific sections within those documents.

3. **Semantic Search - Large**: For very large reference documents like accounting standards, we implement vector embedding similarity search with sophisticated reranking and section expansion. This method performs similarity matching with query embeddings, reranks results based on relevance criteria, and expands selected sections to maintain context.

We select the optimal input pipeline and retrieval method for each database based on document type and structure. This multi-tiered approach allows us to handle various document types optimally rather than applying a one-size-fits-all solution, resulting in more accurate and relevant responses.

We evaluated several alternative approaches before selecting our hybrid architecture:

1. **Basic RAG**: Standard retrieval-augmented generation doesn't apply equally well to varying document types. Tuning for one database might work well but wouldn't be an apply-all solution. This approach would also require querying all databases for every question rather than strategic querying.

2. **Pure LLM Approach**: Using a large language model alone would risk providing information not backed by official policy documentation and make source attribution difficult, making it inappropriate for financial policy guidance where attribution is essential.

3. **Single-Stage Processing**: A non-agent approach would limit the system's ability to make strategic decisions about information retrieval and would reduce transparency in the reasoning process.

The selected approach aligns with the model's purpose of providing accurate, policy-compliant guidance based on authorized knowledge sources while maintaining transparency and efficiency.

4.2 Model Inputs

IRIS uses a streamlined input approach centered around conversation history and structured database content. The key inputs include:
• **Conversation History**: The primary input is the complete conversation history, including the current user query and all previous exchanges. This history provides essential context for understanding the current query and enables continuity in multi-turn conversations, especially for follow-up questions or references to previously discussed topics.
• **PostgreSQL Database Content**: The system connects directly to PostgreSQL tables (as defined in the postgres_schema.sql file) that store processed document content and metadata. These tables provide the knowledge base from which relevant information is retrieved during the research process.
• **Specialized System Prompts**: Throughout the processing pipeline, carefully crafted system prompts are injected to guide each LLM component. These prompts define the role and expected output format for each agent and vary based on the specific task (routing, clarification, planning, etc.).

The agent architecture processes the conversation history by strategically injecting specialized system prompts at each stage and incorporating relevant content retrieved from the PostgreSQL database as context for the LLM. This approach allows the system to maintain conversation context while providing accurate, document-grounded responses to finance and accounting policy inquiries.

Our solution leverages industry best practices and public benchmarking/leaderboard results to select appropriate models and approaches. The hybrid approach, with its flexible document processing and retrieval methods, worked effectively with minimal adjustments beyond tuning prompts and optimizing database storage.

The IRIS system retrieves information from a collection of internal and external accounting and finance knowledge sources stored in a PostgreSQL database. This section describes the data used by the system, its sources, and processing methods.

Data Coverage and Scope

IRIS accesses data covering the following domains:

This domain-focused coverage enables IRIS to address finance and accounting inquiries relevant to the CFO Group.

Data Storage and Management

The system stores processed documents in a PostgreSQL database with a structured schema designed to support both catalog-based and semantic search approaches. The database consists of three primary tables:

**1. apg_catalog**: Document Metadata Repository
CREATE TABLE apg_catalog (
    -- System Fields
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,                        -- Auto-incrementing unique identifier
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT NOW(), -- When the record was added to the database
    
    -- Document Identification Fields
    document_source VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,        -- Source of the document (e.g., 'internal_capm', 'external_iasb')
    document_type VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,          -- Type of document (e.g., 'capm', 'infographic', 'memo')
    document_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,          -- Formatted document name (e.g., 'IFRS 9 - Financial Instruments')
    
    -- Scope Fields
    document_description TEXT,                    -- Original AI-generated description of document usage/scope
    document_usage TEXT,                          -- Field for LLM selection/usage guidance
    
    -- Refresh Metadata Fields
    date_created TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE,        -- Original document creation date
    date_last_modified TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE,  -- Date the document was last modified
    file_name VARCHAR(255),                       -- Full filename with extension (e.g., 'IFRS9_Financial_Instruments.pdf')
    file_type VARCHAR(50),                        -- File extension/type (e.g., '.pdf', '.docx', '.xlsx')
    file_size BIGINT,                             -- Size of the file in bytes
    file_path VARCHAR(1000),                      -- Full system path to the original file
    file_link VARCHAR(1000)                       -- URL or NAS path to the file
)

**2. apg_content**: Document Content Storage
CREATE TABLE apg_content (
    -- System Fields
    id SERIAL PRIMARY KEY,                        -- Auto-incrementing unique identifier
    created_at TIMESTAMP WITH TIME ZONE DEFAULT NOW(), -- When the record was added to the database
    
    -- Document Reference Fields (matching catalog)
    document_source VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,        -- Source of the document (matches apg_catalog)
    document_type VARCHAR(100) NOT NULL,          -- Type of document (matches apg_catalog)
    document_name VARCHAR(255) NOT NULL,          -- Document name (matches apg_catalog)
    
    -- Content Fields
    section_id INTEGER NOT NULL,                  -- Ordered sequence number within the document
    section_name VARCHAR(500),                    -- Title of the section/chapter
    section_summary TEXT,                         -- AI-generated summary of the section
    section_content TEXT NOT NULL                 -- The actual content of the section
)

**3. iris_textbook_database**: Vector Embeddings Store
CREATE TABLE iris_textbook_database (
  -- SYSTEM FIELDS

6.2 Database Retrieval Methodologies

The IRIS system implements five distinct database retrieval methodologies, each optimized for different types of source documents:

6.2.1 Catalog Search - Small

**Mathematical Specification:**
• Two-stage selection with catalog prioritization
• Document assessment function: $R_{LLM}(d, q) \rightarrow \{d_1, d_2, ..., d_n\}$ representing LLM-selected documents
• Document selection is modeled as: $D_{selected} = LLM_{select}(catalog, q)$ where the LLM selects documents based on title and description
• Response synthesis: $R_{synthesis} = LLM_{synthesize}(\{content(d) | d \in D_{selected}\}, q)$

**Implementation:**
1. **Catalog Retrieval**: Query PostgreSQL for document metadata using SQL:
      SELECT id, document_name, document_description
   FROM apg_catalog
   WHERE document_source = 'internal_par'
   ORDER BY document_name
   
2. **Document Selection**: LLM-based selection (GPT-4o-mini with temperature 0.2):
• Format catalog with document IDs, names, and descriptions
• Pass to LLM with selection prompt
• Parse returned JSON list of document IDs
• Fallback regex extraction if JSON parsing fails
3. **Content Retrieval**: Fetch complete document content for selected IDs:
      SELECT section_id, section_name, section_content
   FROM apg_content
   WHERE document_source = 'internal_par'
   AND document_name = %s
   ORDER BY section_id
   
4. **Response Synthesis**: Generate research report using tool call (GPT-4o with temperature 0.2):
• Format documents with hierarchical structure: # Document Name → ## Section Name → Content
• Tool call returns both status_summary and detailed_research
• Citations include document name and section name

**Application:**
• Used for standard documents (PDF, DOCX) with simple structure
• Example databases: internal_par, internal_process_and_controls

6.2.2 Catalog Search - Large

**Mathematical Specification:**
• Three-stage hierarchical selection using LLM judgment
• Document selection: $D_{selected} = LLM_{select}(catalog, q)$ where the LLM selects relevant documents
• Section selection: $S_{selected,d} = LLM_{select}(sections(d), q)$ for each $d \in D_{selected}$
• Final synthesis: $R_{synthesis} = LLM_{synthesize}(\{content(s) | s \in \bigcup_{d \in D_{selected}} S_{selected,d}\}, q)$

**Implementation:**
1. **Catalog Retrieval**: Same SQL query pattern as Catalog Search - Small but for CAPM sources
2. **Document Selection**: LLM-based selection (GPT-4o-mini with temperature 0.2):
• Same process as Catalog Search - Small
• Returns list of document IDs from the catalog
3. **Section Summary Retrieval**: Additional intermediate step:
      SELECT section_id, section_name, section_summary
   FROM apg_content
   WHERE document_source = 'internal_capm'
   AND document_name = %s
   ORDER BY section_id
   
4. **Section Selection**: LLM-based selection of specific sections (GPT-4o-mini):
• Format document sections and their summaries
• LLM selects specific section IDs for each document based on relevance to query
• Returns dictionary mapping document names to lists of section IDs
5. **Content Retrieval**: Fetch only selected sections:
      SELECT section_id, section_name, section_content
   FROM apg_content
   WHERE document_source = 'internal_capm'
   AND document_name = %s
   AND section_id IN (%s, %s, ...)
   ORDER BY section_id
   
6. **Response Synthesis**: Same tool-calling approach as Catalog Search - Small

**Application:**
• Used for large documents with distinct sections/chapters
• Example databases: internal_capm, internal_global_finance_standards

6.2.3 Catalog Search - Excel

**Mathematical Specification:**
• Table preprocessing with row-level document conversion
• Each row/grouping $r$ is transformed to document $d_r$ by function $T_{excel}(r) = d_r$
• Document selection through LLM assessment: $D_{selected} = LLM_{select}(catalog, q)$ where each catalog entry represents a processed row/table

**Implementation:**
1. **Pre-Processing**: Excel files are processed during database creation:
• Excel content is extracted and converted to Markdown format
• Each table or logical row grouping becomes a separate document
• Tables are preserved with Markdown formatting
2. **Database Query**: After pre-processing, same implementation as Catalog Search - Small:
• Document selection via LLM from catalog
• Content retrieval for selected documents
• Response synthesis using GPT-4o tool call

**Application:**
• Used for Excel spreadsheets and tabular data
• Example databases: internal_wiki, internal_management_reporting

6.2.4 Catalog Search - Vision

**Mathematical Specification:**
• Image-to-text conversion using multi-pass vision transformer
• Image content extraction: $T_{vision}(i) = d_i$ where $i$ is an image and $d_i$ is the extracted text document
• Document selection through LLM assessment: $D_{selected} = LLM_{select}(catalog, q)$ 

**Implementation:**
1. **Pre-Processing**: Infographics are processed during database creation:
• Images are processed via Qwen2 vision model in multi-pass mode
• Multiple extraction passes: text, tables, charts, diagrams
• Content is converted to structured Markdown format
• Each infographic becomes a document in the database
2. **Database Query**: After pre-processing, identical implementation to Catalog Search - Small:
• Document selection via LLM from catalog (GPT-4o-mini)
• Content retrieval for selected documents
• Response synthesis using GPT-4o tool call (temperature 0.2)

**Application:**
• Used for infographics and image-based documents
• Example database: internal_cheatsheets

6.2.5 Semantic Search - Large

**Mathematical Specification:**
• Vector-based similarity search with token-aware chunking
• Chunk embedding: $E(c) = \vec{v_c}$ where $c$ is a chunk and $\vec{v_c}$ is its embedding vector
• Query embedding: $E(q) = \vec{v_q}$
• Initial similarity scoring: $sim(c, q) = 1 - (c.embedding <=> q.embedding)$ using the PostgreSQL vector operator
• Initial chunk selection: $C_{initial}(q) = top\_k(C, sim, k)$ where $k = 20$ (INITIAL_K constant)
• Relevance filtering: $C_{filtered} = \{c \in C_{initial} | LLM_{relevance}(c.summary, q) = 1\}$
• Importance reranking: $score(c) = sim(c, q) \cdot (1 + IMPORTANCE\_FACTOR \cdot c.importance\_score)$ where $IMPORTANCE\_FACTOR = 0.2$
• Section expansion: Expand sections with tokens $\leq THRESHOLD$ (with different thresholds based on rank)
• Gap filling: Fill sequence gaps where $0 < gap \leq MAX\_SEQUENCE\_GAP$ (where $MAX\_SEQUENCE\_GAP = 8$)

**Implementation:**
1. **Query embedding generation**: Using text-embedding-3-large (2000 dimensions) to create a dense vector representation of the query
2. **Initial vector similarity search**: Retrieval of top-20 (INITIAL_K) most similar chunks by cosine similarity using PostgreSQL vector extension with the query:
      SELECT c.*, 1 - (c.embedding <=> %s::vector) AS vector_score
   FROM iris_textbook_database c
   WHERE c.document_id = %s
   ORDER BY vector_score DESC
   LIMIT %s;
   
3. **Summary relevance filtering**: LLM-based classification of chunk summaries as relevant (1) or irrelevant (0):
• Uses GPT-4o-mini (temperature 0.2) to classify each chunk's summary
• Removes chunks classified as irrelevant (0)
• Keeps original rank order from vector similarity
4. **Importance-based reranking**:
• Applies formula: $score = vector\_score \cdot (1 + 0.2 \cdot section\_importance\_score)$
• Sorts results by new score (descending)
• Assigns new rank based on this ordering
5. **Section expansion by token count**:
• For top 5 ranked chunks: Expands if section token count ≤ 8000 tokens
• For other chunks: Expands if section token count ≤ 4000 tokens
• Fetches all chunks from the same section when expansion criteria are met
• Groups chunks from the same section together
6. **Sequence gap filling**:
• Identifies sequence gaps between consecutive chunks ≤ 8 sequence numbers
• Fills these gaps by retrieving the intermediate chunks
• Assigns importance scores based on averaging adjacent chunks
7. **Card formatting and response synthesis**:
• Formats all chunks into "cards" with metadata and content
• Generates comprehensive research report using GPT-4o (temperature 0.7)
• Synthesis must include inline citations from the source material

**Application:**
• Used for very large documents and external sources
• Example databases: external_ey, external_iasb, external_kpmg, external_pwc

6.5 Numerical Methods and Approximations

6.5.1 Tokenization
• Tokenizer: tiktoken (cl100k_base)
• Token thresholds:
• Context window management: Dynamic truncation based on token counts
• Chat history: Maintains last 10 messages within token limit
• Document chunking: 250-750 tokens per chunk (depending on semantic boundaries)

6.5.2 Token-Aware Chunking Algorithm

Section-aware chunking implements a numerical approximation for optimal information density through a hierarchical sectional chunking process:

**Mathematical Formulation:**
• Let $D$ represent a document consisting of sections $S = \{s_1, s_2, ..., s_n\}$
• For each section $s_i$, calculate token count $T(s_i)$
• Define threshold parameters $T_{min} = 250$ and $T_{max} = 750$
• The chunking process can be expressed as a sequence of operations:
• If $T(s_i) < T_{min}$: Merge with adjacent section $s_{i+1}$ if $T(s_i) + T(s_{i+1}) \leq T_{max}$
• If $T(s_i) > T_{max}$: Split into subsections $\{s_{i,1}, s_{i,2}, ..., s_{i,k}\}$ such that $T_{min} \leq T(s_{i,j}) \leq T_{max}$ for all $j \in \{1,2,...,k\}$
• If $T_{min} \leq T(s_i) \leq T_{max}$: Maintain as a standalone chunk

**Implementation Process:**
1. The system traverses the document structure hierarchically, beginning with major sections
2. For each section, the tokenizer calculates precise token counts using tiktoken
3. Section boundaries are preserved where possible to maintain semantic coherence
4. When sections exceed the maximum token threshold, the system splits at paragraph boundaries
5. When sections are below the minimum threshold, they are merged with adjacent sections
6. Merged sections are continuously evaluated against the maximum threshold
7. The final chunking preserves the document's logical flow while maintaining optimal chunk sizes

This approach balances information density with semantic coherence, ensuring that related content remains together while maintaining manageable chunk sizes for embedding and retrieval.

6.5.3 Embedding Batch Processing

To optimize embedding generation, the system implements vector batch processing through a parallelized computation architecture:

**Mathematical Formulation:**
• Given a set of chunks $C = \{c_1, c_2, ..., c_m\}$
• Define batch size $B = 50$
• Partition $C$ into batch sets $B_i = \{c_{i \cdot B + 1}, c_{i \cdot B + 2}, ..., c_{min((i+1) \cdot B, m)}\}$ for $i \in \{0, 1, ..., \lceil m/B \rceil - 1\}$
• Apply embedding function $E$ to each batch: $E(B_i) = \{E(c) | c \in B_i\}$
• The complete embedding set is the union: $E(C) = \bigcup_{i=0}^{\lceil m/B \rceil - 1} E(B_i)$

**Implementation Process:**
1. The system divides the total chunk collection into batches of 50 chunks each
2. Each batch is submitted to the embedding API as a single request
3. The embedding model (text-embedding-3-large) processes each chunk in the batch
4. Returned embeddings are stored with their corresponding chunk IDs
5. The system monitors embedding generation for quality and consistency
6. Embeddings are stored in the PostgreSQL database using the pgvector extension

This batched approach significantly improves throughput by reducing API overhead while maintaining embedding quality and consistency.

6.5.4 Similarity Scoring

Vector similarity is calculated using cosine similarity:

$$sim(v_1, v_2) = \frac{v_1 \cdot v_2}{||v_1|| \cdot ||v_2||}$$

Where $v_1$ and $v_2$ are embedding vectors for chunks or queries.

The IRIS system operates through a multi-tier architecture:

The system functions as follows:

1. **Maven Interface**: Users interact with the Maven UI, which manages:
• User authentication and access control through Active Directory groups

• Conversation history and session management
• User interface presentation and streaming of responses

2. **IRIS Model API**: The IRIS model code functions as an API and:
• Receives conversation context and authorized database access from Maven
• Processes queries through the agent framework
• Returns streaming responses back to Maven
• Logs process monitoring data for performance tracking

3. **Database Management**:
• Separate PostgreSQL tables for knowledge retrieval and process monitoring

4. **LLM Integration**:
• IRIS connects to language models through RBC's Lumina portal

Currently, the IRIS code has been implemented and deployed in Dataiku as a Dash web application demo. This same code is being integrated into the Maven UI for the production implementation. Both implementations interact with the same PostgreSQL database, with no changes being made during the transition from Dataiku to Maven.

7.1.2 Agent Framework

The agent system is implemented as a collection of Python modules, each responsible for specific aspects of query processing as described in Section 6.1:
• **Router Agent**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/agent_router/router.py, determines the optimal processing path for each query by analyzing query intent and required knowledge sources.
• **Clarifier Agent**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/agent_clarifier/clarifier.py, ensures queries contain sufficient context before research begins by identifying and requesting missing information.
• **Planner Agent**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/agent_planner/planner.py, creates research plans by selecting appropriate knowledge sources and setting search parameters.
• **Database Subagents**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/database_subagents/, specialized modules for retrieving information from specific knowledge sources:
• Internal sources (CAPM, Cheatsheets, Compliance, etc.)
• External sources (EY, IASB, KPMG, PwC)
• **Summarizer Agent**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/agent_summarizer/summarizer.py, synthesizes findings into coherent, properly cited responses.
• **Direct Response Agent**: Implemented in /iris/src/agents/agent_direct_response/response_from_conversation.py, generates responses based solely on conversation history without database access.

Data flows between agents through structured JSON messages containing the query context, research parameters, retrieved information, and synthesized responses. Each agent maintains its own state during processing and passes relevant information to downstream agents.

7.1.3 Knowledge Store Implementation

The IRIS knowledge store is implemented as a PostgreSQL database with the following structure:

Core Database Tables

1. **apg_catalog**: Document metadata repository
• Document identification (source, type, name)
• Document description and usage information
• File metadata (creation date, modification date, size, path)
   
2. **apg_content**: Document content storage
• References to documents in apg_catalog
• Section-based content organization
• Section summaries and full content text

3. **iris_textbook_database**: Vector-enabled document chunk storage
• Document and section structural positioning (as detailed in Section 5)
• Chapter-level metadata with tags and summaries
• Section-level pagination and importance scoring
• Content text and 2000-dimension vector embeddings
• Full-text search vector for text-based queries

4. **process_monitor_logs**: Performance tracking system
• Detailed logging of each model invocation
• Timing and token usage metrics for each processing stage
• LLM call details stored as structured JSON

Database Connectivity

The system connects to the database through a standardized configuration layer in /iris/src/initial_setup/db_config.py, which:
• Provides connection parameters for both local development and RBC environments
• Handles connection management and error recovery
• Verifies the existence of required tables

Database Processing Methods

The IRIS system employs five distinct approaches for knowledge retrieval (as detailed in Section 6.2):

1. **Catalog Search - Small**: Used for standard documents with simple structure
• Two-stage selection with catalog prioritization
• Document selection through LLM evaluation of metadata
• Full section retrieval for selected documents
• Used for databases like internal_par and internal_process_and_controls

2. **Catalog Search - Large**: Used for large documents with hierarchical structure
• Three-stage hierarchical selection using LLM judgment
• Section summary evaluation for targeted content retrieval
• Used for databases like internal_capm and internal_global_finance_standards

3. **Catalog Search - Excel**: Used for tabular data sources
• Table preprocessing with row-level document conversion
• Table format preservation in Markdown
• Used for databases like internal_wiki and internal_management_reporting

4. **Catalog Search - Vision**: Used for infographics and visual content
• Image-to-text conversion using multi-pass vision transformer
• Multi-pass extraction of text, tables, and diagrams
• Used for visual content in internal_cheatsheets

5. **Semantic Search - Large**: Used for extensive reference materials
• Vector-based similarity search with token-aware chunking
• Sophisticated reranking based on importance scores and vector similarity
• Section expansion and sequence gap filling
• Used for external sources like external_ey, external_iasb, external_kpmg, and external_pwc

The database subagents implement these different approaches based on the specific requirements of each knowledge source, with a central database router (database_router.py) directing queries to the appropriate subagent.

8.2.2 Identified Issues and Resolutions

Through the iterative testing process, several issues were identified and addressed. The following represents key issues from actual business feedback:

   **Note:** Detailed feedback from APG reviewers, including issue reports and suggested improvements, is available in the SharePoint folder: https://rbcds.sharepoint.com/sites/IRISProject/Shared Documents/TestArtifacts/APGFeedback/

1. **Unsolicited IFRS vs. GAAP Comparisons**: 
• **Issue**: The system included IFRS vs. GAAP comparisons even when not requested, making responses unnecessarily long.
• **Resolution**: Enhanced prompt instructions for the CAPM subagent to focus only on the standard relevant to the query (defaulting to IFRS if none specified) and actively filter out information about other standards unless explicitly requested.

2. **Incomplete Answers Due to Sequential Database Processing**:
• **Issue**: Responses sometimes contained information only from one database (e.g., CAPM) when relevant information also existed in others (e.g., APG Wiki).
• **Resolution**: Implemented parallel database querying and updated the Summarizer agent to synthesize findings from all sources into a comprehensive response.

3. **Overly Verbose Response Format**:
• **Issue**: Responses included excessive internal processing details like "Thought", "Clarify", "Researching", making outputs long and difficult to follow.
• **Resolution**: Eliminated internal "thinking" steps from display, streamlined clarification and research status updates, and configured the Summarizer to generate only the final, synthesized report.

4. **Incorrect Handling of Finance-Related Queries**:
• **Issue**: Basic finance concept definitions were sometimes answered directly without database research, risking responses based on the model's training data.
• **Resolution**: Updated the Router agent to explicitly route all finance-related queries (including basic definitions) to the research path, and enhanced the Direct Response agent to refuse answering finance queries without prior research results.

5. **Missing Critical Information**:
• **Issue**: Specific, critical pieces of information (like conditions or criteria) were sometimes missing from responses.
• **Resolution**: Enhanced prompts for subagents to accurately extract specific conditions or tests, and for the Summarizer to preserve these details in the final synthesis.

6. **Unnecessary Detail in Responses**:
• **Issue**: Even when asking about specific aspects (e.g., classification of financial assets), responses included details on unrelated aspects (e.g., measurement, impairment).
• **Resolution**: Implemented adaptive synthesis logic to match the scope of the response to the specific user request, providing concise answers for targeted questions.

7. **Inconsistent Spacing/Formatting**:
• **Issue**: Response formatting showed inconsistencies in spacing, indentation, and Markdown usage.
• **Resolution**: Enhanced formatting instructions for the Summarizer agent with explicit guidelines for handling headings, lists, paragraph spacing, and proper Markdown structure.

8. **Incorrect Labeling of Source Authority**:
• **Issue**: External firm guidance (EY, KPMG, PwC) was incorrectly labeled as "authoritative" when only standards bodies like IASB are authoritative.
• **Resolution**: Updated source descriptions to clearly label external firm guidance as "External Supplementary" and IASB as "External Authoritative," ensuring correct source hierarchy understanding.

8.2.3 User Interface Issues Addressed

Several issues related to the user interface were also identified and addressed:

1. **Inability to Scroll During Response Streaming**:
• **Issue**: Users couldn't scroll up to view previous messages during response generation.
• **Resolution**: Modified the UI code to allow scrolling during streaming while maintaining auto-scroll after completion.

2. **Non-functional Feedback Controls**:
• **Issue**: Thumbs up/down icons didn't function in the test environment.
• **Resolution**: Clarified that these features are planned for the production implementation.

3. **Graphical Content Display**:
• **Issue**: Users requested the ability to see actual graphical cheat sheets rather than text descriptions.
• **Resolution**: Implemented multi-pass vision preprocessing to extract detailed content from graphical elements while acknowledging the current text-only display limitation.

3. **No Formal Benchmark Comparison**: Alternative RAG architectures or methods were not explicitly tested as comparisons, as method selection was based on prior experience and project requirements.

4. **Limited Load Testing**: While basic functionality was confirmed, extensive load or stress testing to identify performance boundaries was not conducted.

5. **Subject Matter Expert Availability**: Testing relied on available subject matter experts rather than a formal, diverse panel of evaluators.

6. **Testing Environment Limitations**: Testing has been conducted primarily in the Dataiku environment, with full Maven integration testing pending.

These limitations are considered acceptable given the nature of the project and its intended use as an internal tool with human oversight.

2. **Knowledge Boundaries**: The system appropriately identifies when information is outside its knowledge base and provides appropriate disclaimers, as confirmed through negative testing.

Based on the testing results, the IRIS system is considered suitable for deployment as a finance and accounting policy guidance tool, with the understanding that human oversight remains essential for verifying responses on critical matters. Section 10: Evaluation of Model Uncertainty

10.1 Uncertainty Assessment Approach

For the IRIS model, we have conducted a structured uncertainty assessment to quantify the level of uncertainty in model outputs and determine whether this uncertainty is acceptable given the model's intended use as a finance and accounting policy guidance tool.

Our assessment combines quantitative metrics from our testing results with qualitative analysis of uncertainty management mechanisms built into the system. This approach aligns with the requirements for models of medium materiality, focusing on:

1. Quantifying uncertainty through empirical testing results
2. Analyzing system behavior in edge cases and ambiguous scenarios
3. Evaluating the system's ability to express appropriate confidence levels
4. Determining if the uncertainty is acceptable for the intended business use

10.1.1 Uncertainty Measurement Methodology

We have employed multiple complementary approaches to measure uncertainty:

1. **Accuracy Metrics**: Using our quantitative testing results as inverse proxies for uncertainty
2. **Error Analysis**: Analyzing error patterns and failure modes in test cases
3. **Confidence Signaling Assessment**: Evaluating how the system communicates its confidence levels
4. **Edge Case Performance**: Testing behavior with ambiguous or out-of-scope queries

10.1.2 Sources of Uncertainty

In the IRIS system, uncertainty can arise from several sources:

1. **Database Selection Uncertainty**: The Router's ability to select the appropriate knowledge sources
2. **Information Retrieval Uncertainty**: The system's ability to identify relevant documents and passages
3. **Information Synthesis Uncertainty**: The Summarizer's ability to accurately combine information from multiple sources
4. **Disambiguation Uncertainty**: The Clarifier's ability to recognize and resolve ambiguous queries
5. **Knowledge Boundary Uncertainty**: The system's ability to recognize when information is unavailable or outside its scope

10.2 Quantitative Uncertainty Assessment

Based on our comprehensive testing with both APG and non-APG databases, we have derived the following uncertainty measures:

10.2.1 Core Component Uncertainty

10.2.3 Overall Uncertainty Assessment

Based on the quantitative measures above, the IRIS model demonstrates an overall uncertainty range of ±10.4%, with higher certainty in upstream components (database and document selection) and slightly lower certainty in response synthesis.

Given that the model is used as a research and guidance tool with explicit verification requirements rather than for direct decision-making, this level of uncertainty is assessed as **ACCEPTABLE** for the intended business purpose.

10.3 Uncertainty Management Mechanisms

The IRIS system implements several mechanisms to appropriately manage and communicate uncertainty:

10.3.1 Router Agent Uncertainty Management
• Finance-related queries are explicitly routed to research rather than direct response
• For ambiguous queries, the system follows a "when in doubt, research" approach
• A secondary safety layer exists in the Direct Response agent, which refuses to answer finance-related questions without research

10.3.2 Clarifier Agent Uncertainty Recognition
• Ambiguous or underspecified queries trigger specific clarification requests
• For standard selection (e.g., IFRS vs. GAAP), the system defaults to IFRS while explicitly indicating this assumption
• Query intention ambiguity is surfaced rather than making potentially incorrect assumptions

10.3.3 Response Synthesis Uncertainty Communication
• The system explicitly acknowledges contradictions between different information sources
• Authoritative source hierarchies are clearly established (IASB for standards, CAPM for internal policy)
• All responses include disclaimers about verification requirements and the need for professional judgment

10.3.4 Knowledge Boundary Communication
• The system clearly states when questions fall outside available knowledge sources
• Temporal limitations of information are acknowledged, especially for recently updated standards
• Document metadata including last updated dates are incorporated in responses where relevant

10.4 Acceptability of Uncertainty

The uncertainty level in the IRIS model is deemed acceptable based on several factors:

1. **Business Context**: The model serves as a research assistant rather than an automated decision-maker, with all outputs subject to human verification
2. **Risk Mitigation**: Clear disclaimers, source attribution, and verification statements mitigate the risk of incorrect information being acted upon
3. **Comparative Performance**: The model achieves accuracy comparable to or exceeding manual reference processes while providing enhanced consistency
4. **Balanced Tradeoffs**: The model balances coverage (breadth of knowledge) with accuracy (depth and correctness)
5. **Continuous Monitoring**: Ongoing uncertainty assessment ensures any drift in performance is quickly identified

10.4.1 Materiality Assessment

Given that the model is used internally by finance professionals who are required to verify information against primary sources, the uncertainty present in the model is assessed as:
• **Low Impact**: Users understand the tool provides research assistance, not definitive answers
• **Low Likelihood of Harm**: Clear source attributions allow verification of important information
• **Non-Critical Application**: The model is not used for regulatory filing or external reporting
• **Transparent Limitations**: All users are made aware of system limitations through training and in-system disclaimers

10.4.2 Conclusion on Uncertainty

Based on the quantitative uncertainty estimates and the model's intended use, we conclude that:

1. The overall uncertainty level of ±10.4% is ACCEPTABLE for the model's business purpose
2. The uncertainty is appropriately managed through built-in mechanisms
3. The uncertainty is transparently communicated to users
4. Ongoing monitoring will ensure uncertainty remains within acceptable bounds

10.5 Ongoing Uncertainty Monitoring

The uncertainty profile of the system will be continuously monitored through our quarterly evaluation process:
• The 50-question evaluation set includes specifically designed edge cases to test boundary recognition
• The same quantitative metrics (database selection, document selection, answer accuracy) will be calculated quarterly
• Any trend indicating increasing uncertainty will trigger a detailed analysis and potential model adjustments
• User feedback from the Maven interface provides real-world validation of appropriate confidence signaling

By maintaining consistent evaluation metrics between the initial testing and ongoing monitoring, we will track any changes in the system's uncertainty over time, ensuring it remains within acceptable bounds for its intended use.

Section 11: Further Research

Based on our experience developing and testing the IRIS system, we have identified several promising avenues for further research and enhancement. These areas represent clear paths for continued development based on both the strengths and limitations observed in the current implementation.

11.1 Model Enhancement Opportunities
• **LLM Model Evaluation**: Regularly assess newer LLM models alongside our current OpenAI implementations to evaluate potential performance improvements in reasoning capabilities, response quality, and processing efficiency.
• **Response Optimization**: Further refine response generation to balance comprehensiveness with conciseness, ensuring answers directly address user queries without unnecessary detail while maintaining appropriate context and citation.
• **Source Conflict Resolution**: Enhance the system's ability to identify, reconcile, and clearly present information when different knowledge sources contain potentially conflicting guidance, with explicit indication of source authority hierarchies.
• **Latency Reduction**: Continue optimizing the multi-stage processing pipeline to reduce end-to-end response times while maintaining comprehensive information retrieval, particularly for complex research queries spanning multiple knowledge domains.

11.2 Knowledge Base Expansion
• **Content Coverage**: Systematically expand database content based on identified knowledge gaps and user feedback, prioritizing high-demand finance and accounting policy areas that currently have limited coverage.
• **Document Currency Management**: Develop enhanced approaches for tracking and surfacing document recency information, ensuring users understand the temporal context of the information provided.
• **Visual Content Handling**: Improve the processing and presentation of visual elements from source documents, particularly for complex financial diagrams, flowcharts, and infographics from cheat sheets and technical publications.
• **Reference Hyperlinking**: Explore mechanisms for implementing direct hyperlinks to source documents within responses, integrated with appropriate access controls and permission management.

11.3 System Architecture Improvements
• **Router Refinement**: Further enhance the router component to improve database selection accuracy, particularly for queries that span multiple knowledge domains or that involve emerging finance topics.
• **Clarifier Enhancement**: Expand the clarifier agent's capabilities to better handle ambiguous queries and identify more subtle cases where additional context is needed for accurate research.
• **Database Refresh Optimization**: Streamline the document ingestion and refresh pipeline to support more frequent updates and larger volumes of documents while maintaining metadata quality.
• **Interpretability Improvements**: Implement enhanced logging of intermediate reasoning steps to improve system auditability and enable more detailed analysis of decision processes.

11.4 Evaluation and Monitoring Advancement
• **Automated Evaluation**: Develop more sophisticated approaches for automated performance assessment to supplement manual evaluation processes, potentially incorporating LLM-based judging of response quality.
• **Test Suite Expansion**: Create additional targeted test questions focusing on edge cases, source conflicts, and emerging finance topics to continuously verify model robustness.
• **User Feedback Integration**: Refine mechanisms for collecting, analyzing, and acting upon user feedback data from the Maven interface, including both explicit feedback (thumbs up/down) and implicit signals.
• **Performance Tracking Dashboards**: Implement comprehensive visualization and tracking of system performance metrics over time, enabling early identification of potential drift or degradation.

11.5 User Experience Enhancements
• **Response Formatting**: Continue refining the presentation of complex financial information, particularly tables, lists, and hierarchical concepts, to improve readability and comprehension.
• **Conversation Flow**: Enhance the system's ability to maintain context across multi-turn conversations, especially for complex research topics requiring progressive refinement.
• **Multi-language Support**: Evaluate approaches for supporting additional languages in both queries and knowledge sources, expanding accessibility for global finance teams.

These research directions will be pursued based on operational priorities, user feedback, and evolving business requirements. Regular assessment of the implemented enhancements will ensure continued alignment with the system's core purpose of providing accurate, comprehensive finance and accounting policy guidance.
